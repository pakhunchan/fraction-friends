Quiz Variant Comparison

4 quiz styles for the equivalent fractions lesson

v1: Visual Comparison — Ember shows two fractions side by side and asks "Are they the same amount or not?" Simple yes/no binary choice. 4 questions total.

Question 1

Ember shows 1/2, then 2/4
Is one-half the same amount as two-fourths?
Yes, same amount! No, they're different
"No, they're different" Hmm, think about it -- if you cut a cake into 4 pieces and take 2 of them, how much cake do you have? Try picturing it in your head and give it another go!

Question 2

Ember shows 1/3, then 2/4
Is one-third the same amount as two-fourths?
Yes, same amount! No, they're different
"Yes, same amount!" Not quite! One-third means the cake is cut into 3 pieces and you take 1. Two-fourths means it's cut into 4 pieces and you take 2. Those are different amounts! Try again.

Question 3

Ember shows 2/4, then 4/8
Is two-fourths the same amount as four-eighths?
Yes, same amount! No, they're different
"No, they're different" Hmm, the numbers look bigger, but think about it -- if you cut the cake into 8 pieces and take 4, that's still half the cake! And two-fourths is also half. Give it another try!

Question 4

Ember shows 1/4, then 1/2
Is one-fourth the same amount as one-half?
Yes, same amount! No, they're different
"Yes, same amount!" Not quite! One-fourth means the cake is cut into 4 pieces and you only get 1 little piece. One-half means it's cut into 2 and you get 1 bigger piece. That's more cake! Try again.
v2: Fill in the Missing Number — Ember shows an equation with a question mark (e.g. 1/2 = ?/4) and the student picks the missing numerator or denominator. 3-choice questions with per-distractor feedback. 4 puzzles total.

Puzzle 1

Shows 1/2 as reference
1/2 = ?/4 -- What number goes where the question mark is?
1 2 3
Chose "1" Not quite! One-fourth is a smaller piece than one-half. Remember, when we cut each half into two more pieces, we get TWICE as many. Think about how many fourths fit in one half, and try again!
Chose "3" Hmm, three-fourths would be more than one-half -- that's too much cake! We need exactly the same amount as one-half. Try again!

Puzzle 2

Shows 2/4 as reference
2/4 = 1/? -- What number is missing on the bottom?
2 3 4
Chose "3" Hmm, one-third would mean cutting into three pieces -- that's a different amount than two-fourths. Remember, two-fourths is the same as one... what? Think about the word we learned today!
Chose "4" One-fourth would be just one small piece out of four. But two-fourths is bigger than that! When you push those two small pieces back together, what do you get? Try again!

Puzzle 3

Shows 1/2 as reference
?/4 = 1/2 -- How many fourths equal one-half?
1 2 3
Chose "1" One-fourth is just one little piece -- that's less than half the cake. If the cake has four pieces, how many do you need to have exactly half? Try again!
Chose "3" Three-fourths would be three pieces out of four -- that's almost the whole cake! That's more than half. Try a smaller number!

Puzzle 4 (stretch)

Shows 1/3 as reference
1/3 = 2/? -- What number goes on the bottom?
4 6 8
Chose "4" Not quite! Think about it this way: the cake starts with 3 big pieces. If you cut EACH of those 3 pieces in half, how many total pieces would you have? That's 3 groups of 2!
Chose "8" That's too many pieces! We start with 3 slices and cut each one into 2. So that's 3 times 2. Can you figure it out? Try again!
v3: Odd One Out — Ember shows three fractions. Two are equivalent and one is not. The student must find the imposter. 3-choice questions with per-distractor feedback. 4 problems total.

Problem 1

Three fractions: 1/2, 2/4, 1/3
Which fraction does NOT belong with the others?
1/2 2/4 1/3
Chose "1/2" Careful, detective! 1/2 and 2/4 are actually the same amount -- remember, two quarters fit perfectly into one half! So they both belong together. The odd one out is something different from one-half. Try again!
Chose "2/4" Not quite! 2/4 is just one-half wearing a disguise -- 2 pieces out of 4 is the same amount as 1 piece out of 2. Those two match! Look for the fraction that ISN'T equal to one-half. Try again!

Problem 2

Three fractions: 2/4, 3/6, 1/4
Which fraction is NOT the same as the others?
2/4 3/6 1/4
Chose "2/4" Hmm, not that one! 2/4 and 3/6 are both secret ways to write one-half. Two out of four pieces is the same as three out of six pieces -- they're equivalent! The odd one out is smaller than one-half. Try again!
Chose "3/6" Close, but 3/6 is actually equal to one-half -- remember the owl's cake? 3 pieces out of 6 is the same as 1 piece out of 2. So 3/6 and 2/4 are twins! Look for the one that's different. Try again!

Problem 3

Three fractions: 1/2, 1/4, 2/4
Which fraction is the odd one out?
1/2 1/4 2/4
Chose "1/2" Careful! 1/2 and 2/4 are equivalent fractions -- they're the same amount! Two pieces out of four equals one piece out of two. So those two match. Which one is left all alone? Try again!
Chose "2/4" Not 2/4! Remember, 2/4 is just another way to write 1/2. They're the same amount of cake! The fraction that doesn't match is the smaller one. Try again!

Problem 4

Three fractions: 2/6, 1/3, 1/2
The owl shows you three fractions. Which one is the odd one out?
2/6 1/3 1/2
Chose "2/6" Tricky, but 2/6 actually belongs here! Think about a cake cut into 6 pieces -- if you take 2, that's the same as taking 1 piece from a cake cut into 3. So 2/6 and 1/3 are the same! The odd one out is bigger. Try again!
Chose "1/3" Almost! But 1/3 and 2/6 are secretly the same amount -- two-sixths is just one-third in disguise! Two tiny pieces out of six equals one bigger piece out of three. The odd one out is the bigger fraction. Try again!
v4: Story Scenarios — Each question is a mini-story about characters (Olive, Benny, Maple, Fern) in the Cozy Kitchen. The student decides whether characters got the same amount or not. 2-3 choice questions with narrative feedback. 4 scenarios total.

Scenario 1: Olive & Benny's Brownies

Olive cut her brownie into 2 equal pieces and ate 1 piece. Benny cut his brownie into 4 equal pieces and ate 2 pieces. Benny says, "Hey, I think you got more than me!"
Olive ate 1/2 of her brownie. Benny ate 2/4 of his brownie. Who ate more?
Olive ate more They ate the same! Benny ate more
"Olive ate more" Hmm, let's think about it. Olive ate 1 piece out of 2. Benny ate 2 pieces out of 4. Remember -- 2 little quarter-pieces fit together to make the same size as 1 half-piece! Try again.
"Benny ate more" It might look like Benny ate more because he had 2 pieces, but those pieces were smaller! Two quarters is the same amount as one half. Let's try that one more time.

Scenario 2: Maple & Fern's Argument

Maple is holding a plate with 2/4 of a brownie. Fern is holding a plate with 1/2 of a brownie. Maple pouts: "That's not fair -- Fern got a bigger piece than me!"
Maple has 2/4 of a brownie. Fern has 1/2 of a brownie. Is Maple right that Fern got more?
Yes, Fern got more No -- they got the same!
"Yes, Fern got more" It does look different, but remember our trick! Two-fourths and one-half are equivalent fractions -- they are the exact same amount. Maple's 2 smaller pieces add up to Fern's 1 bigger piece. Try again!

Scenario 3: The Sharing Plate

Olive takes 2 pieces from a brownie cut into 4. Benny takes 1 piece from a same-size brownie cut into 2.
Olive took 2 out of 4 pieces. Benny took 1 out of 2 pieces. Did they both take the same amount of brownie?
Yes -- both took half! No -- Olive took more
"No -- Olive took more" Olive did take 2 pieces, but her pieces were smaller because the brownie was cut into 4. Two-fourths is the same as one-half! The brownies are the same size, so Olive and Benny each have the same amount. Let's try again.

Scenario 4: Fern's Tricky Question

Fern holds up two cards: one says 1/2, the other says 1/4. "Are THESE two fractions the same amount?"
Is 1/2 the same amount as 1/4?
Yes, they are equal No, they are different!
"Yes, they are equal" Careful! One-half means you cut into 2 pieces and take 1. One-quarter means you cut into 4 pieces and take only 1. That quarter piece is much smaller than a half piece! They are NOT the same. Try again.
